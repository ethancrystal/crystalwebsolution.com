# The Brief and the Deliverables

*The discovery questions I ask. The deliverables I never break.*

---

## Part I — The Discovery Brief

Before designing anything, I ask the client these questions. If they cannot answer them, we are not ready to design. We are still ready to *think.*

### Section 1 — The Customer
1. Describe one specific customer. Not a demographic — a single woman. What is her name, age, profession, neighbourhood?
2. What is currently in her bathroom (specific brands, no generalities)?
3. What is one thing she wishes existed but does not?
4. What does she say to her friend when she recommends a product she loves?
5. What three brands (in any category) does she trust unconditionally?

### Section 2 — The Brand
6. Why did you start this brand? (The honest answer, not the marketing one.)
7. What is the one true thing about your product nobody else can claim?
8. What three adjectives must the brand feel like?
9. What three adjectives must the brand *never* feel like?
10. If your brand were a hotel, which one would it be? (Forces a tier and tone instantly.)

### Section 3 — The Position
11. What price tier? (Mass / Mid / Premium / Luxury)
12. Where will it be sold? (DTC site, Sephora, salons, Instagram-only, all)
13. Who are your three closest competitors — and what do you envy about each?
14. What is your honest differentiator in one sentence?

### Section 4 — The Constraints
15. Where will the logo live most often? (App icon, bottle cap, hero banner, social avatar)
16. What is the smallest size the logo must work at? (Default answer: 32px square)
17. Are there cultural/regional cues that must or must not appear?
18. What is the one element a stakeholder will demand that I should push back on?

These eighteen questions take roughly one hour of conversation. Without them, the work is guessing.

## Part II — The Three Concepts

I never present one option. I never present five. I present **three.**

### Concept 1 — The Safe One
The visual interpretation that obeys all conventions of the brand's tier. Conservative, well-executed, defensible. It will rarely be chosen, but it gives the client confidence that you understood the brief.

### Concept 2 — The Strange One
The visual interpretation that breaks one convention deliberately. It will rarely be chosen, but it expands the client's sense of possibility. Without it, Concept 3 cannot be appreciated.

### Concept 3 — The In-Between
The visual interpretation that takes the structural craft of Concept 1 and inherits *one bold choice* from Concept 2. This is almost always the chosen one. It is the recommended option.

### The Master's Trick
Concept 3 should always be your true recommendation. Concept 2 exists to make Concept 3 look reasonable. Concept 1 exists to make Concept 3 look ambitious.

## Part III — The Deliverables (Never Skip)

Every project ships with all of the following. No exceptions.

### A. The Reasoning Document (1 page)
- The conceptual logic
- The reference stack
- The decisions made and why
- The "what I chose against"

### B. The Primary Lockup
- Vertical lockup
- Horizontal lockup
- Stacked variant
- Each at three sizes (hero, card, cap)

### C. The Secondary Mark
- Monogram or symbolic mark for use where the wordmark cannot fit
- Tested at 32px

### D. The Type System
- Display typeface (with weights)
- Body typeface (with weights)
- Accent / mono if used
- Type pairing rules

### E. The Colour System
- Primary anchor (with hex / Pantone / CMYK / RGB)
- Secondary ink
- One accent (if used)
- Backgrounds and ratios

### F. The Stress Tests
The logo rendered in five real contexts:
1. On the actual product bottle (mockup)
2. As a website hero banner
3. As an Instagram avatar (96px circle)
4. On a tote bag (single-colour print)
5. As a wax seal or stamp (single colour, embossed)

### G. The Single-Colour Test
The logo must work in **one colour** — black on white, white on black. If it breaks at single-colour, it is incomplete.

### H. The Don'ts Sheet
A page showing how the logo must NOT be used:
- Don't stretch
- Don't recolour outside the system
- Don't add drop shadows
- Don't crowd
- Don't pair with disallowed typefaces

### I. Files
- Master vector (SVG, AI, or PDF)
- PNG at 4 sizes (3000px, 1200px, 600px, 96px)
- Single-colour versions (black, white, anchor ink)
- Monogram alone (all sizes)
- The full system as a single PDF brand book

## Part IV — The Conversation, Not the Email

I do not send logo files in an email and ask "what do you think." I get on a call. I walk the client through:

1. The brief I heard (in 90 seconds)
2. The three concepts (3 minutes each)
3. The recommended path (with reasoning)
4. The trade-offs (what we gain, what we choose against)
5. The next two weeks (refinement, stress tests, sign-off)

Logos chosen over email are logos that get changed twice. Logos chosen in conversation are logos that ship.

---

## Part V — Refinement Discipline

After the first round, I follow one rule:

**Refinement is removal. Iteration is addition.**

If a client asks for "more," I ask: *can we instead remove?* If a client wants "more dynamic," I ask: *can we instead earn the pause?* If a client wants "more standout," I ask: *can we instead refuse to compete?*

The best version of any logo is the version that, when you remove one more element, falls apart. Stop one element above falling apart.

---

## The Standard

I have never delivered a logo I would not show in my own portfolio. I have never delivered a logo without all eight stress tests. I have never delivered a logo for a women's brand that I would not be comfortable seeing my own daughter use.

That is the standard. There is no other.
