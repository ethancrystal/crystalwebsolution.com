# Decision Trees — The Master's Frameworks

*The decision-making engine. Use these to choose between options when the brief is ambiguous.*

---

## Decision 1 — Wordmark vs Mark vs Combination?

### Choose **Wordmark Only** when:
- The name is short (1–3 syllables) and memorable
- The brand is editorial or apothecary in positioning
- The budget supports world-class typography
- The brand intends to compete on restraint *(Aesop, Glossier, Hairstory)*

### Choose **Mark + Wordmark Lockup** when:
- The brand will live on bottle caps where the mark must work alone
- The brand needs an Instagram avatar at 96px
- The brand will appear in tight retail spaces where text is illegible
- The brand needs a wax seal, an embossed touch, a stamp *(Oribe, Pattern Beauty)*

### Choose **Mark Alone (very rare)** when:
- The brand has the heritage to support icon-only recognition *(Chanel's interlocking C, Hermès's H)*
- This is almost never available to a new brand. Earn it first.

### Default for new brands:
**Wordmark + supporting monogram in the same identity system.** Wordmark for primary use, monogram for cap/avatar/seal.

## Decision 2 — Serif, Sans, or Script?

### Choose **Serif** when:
- The brand is positioned as editorial, heritage, apothecary, or luxury
- The product is consumed slowly (mask, serum, perfume)
- The audience is 30+ and values considered taste

**Sub-choice:**
- **Didone (Italiana, Didot)** → editorial luxury
- **Old-style serif (Caslon, Garamond)** → literary, intellectual
- **Modern serif (Canela, GT Sectra)** → contemporary considered

### Choose **Sans-Serif** when:
- The brand is positioned as science-led, modern, accessible, or clean-tech
- The product is consumed quickly (shampoo, body wash)
- The audience is 25–40 and values clarity

**Sub-choice:**
- **Humanist sans (Optima, Albertus)** → warm apothecary
- **Geometric sans (Inter, GT Walsheim)** → modern clean
- **Grotesque (Söhne, Founders)** → editorial sans

### Choose **Script** ONLY when:
- It is a real founder signature
- The brand is genuinely artisanal (one chef, one hairstylist, one parfumier)
- The script is paired with maximum restraint everywhere else

If any of those three conditions fail → **do not use script.**

## Decision 3 — All-Caps or Lowercase?

### All-Caps when:
- The brand needs gravitas (Aesop, Oribe, Hairstory)
- The wordmark is short (≤7 letters)
- The brand wants to feel like a *statement*

### Lowercase when:
- The brand wants to feel intimate, modern, downtown (Bumble and bumble, Ouai, Glossier)
- The wordmark has interesting letter shapes that benefit from descenders
- The brand wants to feel like an *insider's secret*

### Mixed Case when:
- The brand has a founder name *(Christophe Robin, Sachajuan)*
- The brand has multiple words and one is clearly primary

## Decision 4 — The Brand Tier Matrix

### MASS (under $15 per unit)
- **Type:** Bold sans-serif, often italic
- **Colour:** Bright, multi-colour
- **Tone:** Loud, promise-driven
- **Reference:** Pantene, Herbal Essences, TRESemmé
- **What to avoid if you don't want to be mass:** Bright multi-colour packaging, exclamation-heavy claims, glossy plastics

### MID ($15–$35 per unit)
- **Type:** Modern sans-serif, sometimes a clean serif
- **Colour:** One signature colour + neutral
- **Tone:** Confident, benefit-driven
- **Reference:** Briogeo, Verb, Function of Beauty, Amika
- **Tip:** This tier rewards design-led restraint more than any other. Most competitors here look cheap. Restraint wins.

### PREMIUM ($35–$70 per unit)
- **Type:** Refined serif or considered humanist sans
- **Colour:** Two-colour disciplined system
- **Tone:** Considered, ingredient-led, science-aware
- **Reference:** Olaplex, Davines, Living Proof, Bumble and bumble, Ouai
- **Tip:** Most clean-beauty brands live here. Avoid the cliché trap (rose-gold + leaf-with-droplet) at all costs.

### LUXURY ($70+ per unit)
- **Type:** Display serif (often Didone) or apothecary humanist
- **Colour:** Single ink + neutral, often black + cream
- **Tone:** Quiet, editorial, almost unbranded
- **Reference:** Aesop, Oribe, Rahua, Kérastase, Le Labo, La Mer
- **Tip:** The luxury tier is defined by what's *removed.* The fewer claims, the smaller the logo, the more confident the brand.

### Rule of Thumb
**Design for the tier above where you currently sit.** A premium brand should look like luxury. A mid brand should look like premium. The visual aspiration is what justifies the price.

## Decision 5 — Colour Palette Logic

### Step 1 — Pick your tier (above)

### Step 2 — Pick your anchor neutral
- Cream `#F2EAD9` (most apothecary)
- Bone `#F4EFE6` (most editorial)
- Fog `#EFE9DD` (most modern-quiet)
- Charcoal `#1F1F1B` (most architectural)

### Step 3 — Pick your ink
- Deep botanical green (clean-beauty default)
- Charcoal-black (editorial)
- Aubergine (Pureology territory — owns the category)
- Terracotta (Mediterranean, warm)

### Step 4 — Pick at most ONE accent
- Antique gold (heritage)
- Dusty rose (soft luxury, used sparingly)
- Sage (clean-beauty)
- Single jewel tone (if owning a category — Pureology purple, Moroccanoil turquoise)

### Stop at 3 colours total.
Three colours = professional. Four = decorative. Five+ = amateur.

## Decision 6 — When to Add a Tagline

### Add a tagline when:
- The brand name is ambiguous about category (AURORA → "Plant-Powered Hair Care" or "Awaken Your Radiance")
- The brand wants to plant a philosophical flag (Hairstory → "wash less, live more")
- The brand needs to signal tier (e.g., "since 1964" for heritage)

### Do NOT add a tagline when:
- The brand name is already self-explanatory (Moroccanoil)
- The brand is intentionally enigmatic (Aesop has no tagline)
- The tagline is forgettable corporate-speak ("Beauty redefined" — never)

### If you use a tagline:
- Italic. Small. One colour. Beneath the wordmark with a hairline or center ornament between.
- ≤ 6 words. Better at 3.
- Read it aloud. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite.

## Decision 7 — Vertical vs Horizontal Lockup

### Vertical (mark stacked on top of wordmark):
- More premium-feeling — implies the brand had room to think
- Better for retail packaging (works on a tall bottle)
- Better for Instagram avatars
- **Default choice for premium and luxury brands**

### Horizontal (mark to the left of wordmark):
- More functional — better for website headers, business cards
- Works at smaller heights
- Less aesthetic gravitas
- **Default choice for mass and mid brands**

### Deliver both. Always. The system needs both for different contexts.

---

## The Master's Final Decision Rule

When in doubt between two options, pick the **quieter** one.

In twenty-five years, I have rarely regretted being too quiet. I have often regretted being too loud.
