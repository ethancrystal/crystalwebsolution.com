# Audience Research, Personas & Jobs-to-be-Done

Goal: a **signal-grounded** picture of who the customer is, what job they're hiring a product to do, and what they'll pay — not a demographic fantasy. Every persona attribute traces to evidence (a review quote, an interview, search data, a community thread). If you can't cite a signal, it's a hypothesis, not a finding — tag it `[hypothesis]`.

## 1. Segment before you persona
Don't describe "the user." Find the **distinct segments** that buy/use for different reasons, then prioritize.
Segment by what actually changes behavior:
- **Job/use-case** (why they hire it) — usually the strongest segmentation axis.
- **Firmographics** (B2B: company size, industry, role, buying process) or **demographics/life-stage** (B2C).
- **Behavior** (frequency, sophistication, current alternative used).
- **Value at stake / willingness-to-pay**.
Prioritize segments on: size (→ market-sizing.md), intensity of pain, ability to reach, and willingness to pay. Pick the **beachhead** — the narrowest segment with the most acute, paid-for pain.

## 2. Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)
People "hire" a product to make progress in a circumstance. Capture the **job**, not the persona's hobbies.

**Job statement format:**
> When I ___ (situation), I want to ___ (motivation), so I can ___ (expected outcome).

Three dimensions of every job:
- **Functional** — the practical task ("consolidate 4 spreadsheets into one report").
- **Emotional** — how they want to feel ("not anxious that the numbers are wrong").
- **Social** — how they want to be perceived ("look on top of it to my boss").

**The four forces** (drives switching — map these for each segment):
1. **Push** of the current situation (what's painful now).
2. **Pull** of the new solution (what's attractive).
3. **Anxiety** about the new (risk, learning curve, switching cost).
4. **Habit/inertia** of the present (why they tolerate the status quo).
Adoption happens only when **Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit**. Your messaging job is to amplify push/pull and reduce anxiety.

**Sourcing JTBD evidence (free):** review mining (esp. "I switched from X because…"), interview "tell me about the last time you…" stories (see [[references/survey-and-interview-design.md]]), Reddit/forum "how do I…" threads, search queries phrased as goals. Quote verbatim.

## 3. Persona brief (only after evidence exists)
A useful persona is a decision tool, not a character sketch. Keep to one page; cite a signal for each line.
```
SEGMENT NAME / one-line who-they-are
- Job-to-be-done (situation → motivation → outcome): ___
- Current alternative / workaround: ___ (and why it falls short)        [signal: ___]
- Top 3 pains (ranked, with evidence quotes + source/date): ___          [signal: ___]
- Desired outcomes / success metric: ___                                 [signal: ___]
- Buying process: who decides, who influences, who pays (B2B)            [signal: ___]
- Where they are reachable (channels/communities): ___                   [signal: ___]
- Willingness to pay / current spend on the job: ___                     [signal: ___]
- Objections / switching anxiety: ___                                    [signal: ___]
CONFIDENCE: H/M/L + why
```
Cut anything that wouldn't change a product, pricing, messaging, or channel decision. Age/gender/hobbies belong only if they demonstrably drive behavior.

## 4. Willingness-to-pay (WTP) — estimation without a big budget
Rank by reliability:
1. **Revealed (strongest)** — what they pay today for the current alternative (incumbent pricing pages, their current tool's invoices, marketplace prices). Past payment > stated intent.
2. **Behavioral tests** — pre-orders, paid waitlist, fake-door pricing page with click-through to "buy."
3. **Stated, structured** — **Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter** (4 questions: too cheap / cheap / expensive / too expensive → maps an acceptable price band) and **Gabor-Granger** (test specific price points → demand curve & revenue-max point).
4. **Stated, casual (weakest)** — "would you pay $X?" People overstate. Discount heavily; never anchor first.
**Value-based framing:** WTP correlates with the *value of the job done* (time saved × hourly cost, revenue gained, risk avoided), not your costs. Quantify the job's value to set a price ceiling. See [[references/positioning-and-pricing.md]].

## 5. Map audience → the rest of the analysis
- Beachhead segment size feeds the **bottom-up SOM** (→ market-sizing.md).
- Pains + unmet jobs feed the **competitive gap** (→ competitive-analysis.md).
- Job + force-map + WTP feed **positioning & pricing** (→ positioning-and-pricing.md).
- Open questions become the **interview/survey** plan (→ survey-and-interview-design.md).

## Anti-patterns
- **Imaginary personas** — "Marketing Mary, 34, loves brunch," with zero supporting signal.
- **Persona theater** — a polished slide nobody uses to make a decision.
- **Demographics as destiny** — age/income that don't predict the buying behavior in question.
- **Asking instead of observing** — stated WTP taken at face value; "would you use this?" answered yes by everyone.
- **One segment to rule them all** — refusing to pick a beachhead and diluting the product for everyone.
- **Ignoring the buyer ≠ user split** (B2B) — designing for the user while the economic buyer has different criteria.
