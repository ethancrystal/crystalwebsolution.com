# Competitive Analysis

Goal: not a list of competitors — a **structured comparison that surfaces a defensible gap** and tells the decision-maker where to play.

## 1. Define the competitive set
- **Direct** — same job, same category (your obvious rivals).
- **Indirect / substitutes** — different solution, same job (incl. spreadsheets, manual processes, "do nothing"). Substitutes are where real budget often sits — never omit them.
- **Adjacent / potential entrants** — players one step away who could move in.
Pick 4–8 for depth. Note selection criteria so the set isn't cherry-picked.

## 2. Teardown framework (per competitor)
Gather from free sources (site, pricing page, docs, G2/Capterra/Trustpilot, app stores, changelog, LinkedIn headcount, job posts, review counts):
- **Positioning** — who they say it's for; their one-line claim; category they name.
- **Target segment** — SMB / mid / enterprise; vertical focus.
- **Core features & notable gaps** — table-stakes vs differentiators.
- **Pricing** — model (seat / usage / flat / freemium), tiers, anchor price, free trial.
- **GTM** — self-serve vs sales-led; primary channels; content/SEO strength.
- **Traction proxies** — review count & rating, app installs, headcount/growth (job posts), funding, social following.
- **Strengths / weaknesses** — from reviews: what users praise, what they complain about (mine the 1–3★ reviews hardest — that's where gaps live).

## 3. Feature / pricing / positioning matrix
Rows = competitors (+ "You" / "Proposed"). Columns = the axes that matter to the *buyer's decision*, not every feature.

```
Competitor | Target segment | Positioning (1-liner) | Pricing model & anchor | Key strengths | Key gaps (from reviews) | Rating (n)
-----------|----------------|------------------------|------------------------|---------------|-------------------------|-----------
A          | SMB            | "all-in-one for X"     | $29/seat/mo            | UX, integrations | slow support, no API | 4.3 (1,204)
B          | Enterprise     | "secure X at scale"    | Custom / sales         | compliance     | expensive, complex   | 4.0 (310)
You        | ...            | ...                    | ...                    | ...           | ...                  | —
```
Keep feature comparisons to **decision-driving** capabilities; a 40-row checklist obscures the signal.

## 4. Positioning map (2×2)
Plot competitors on two axes buyers actually trade off (e.g., *price* × *ease of use*, or *breadth* × *depth-for-a-vertical*). White space on the map = candidate positioning — but only real if **demand exists there** (validate via free-data-sources.md). Empty corners are sometimes empty for a reason.

## 5. Share of voice (free proxies)
Estimate mindshare without paid tools:
- **Search** — Google Trends relative interest by brand term; branded keyword volume (free keyword tools).
- **Community** — mention counts on Reddit/HN/Stack Overflow; sentiment of those mentions.
- **Reviews** — count & velocity of new reviews on G2/Capterra/app stores (proxy for active adoption).
- **Content/SEO** — # ranking pages, backlinks (free-tier of SEO tools), YouTube/social following.
Report SoV as *relative* (Brand A ~3× the search interest of Brand B), dated, with the proxy named — not as precise market share.

## 6. Find the gap → recommendation
Synthesize into: **underserved segment**, **unmet need** (recurring complaint across competitors' reviews), **pricing gap** (over/under-served price point), or **positioning gap** (claim no one owns). Then:
> "Recommendation: position as ___ for ___ segment, because incumbents A/B leave ___ unserved (evidence: ___). Risk: ___ could close this gap by ___."

## Anti-patterns
- Logos on a slide with no axes and no implication.
- Comparing only features you happen to win on (cherry-picked axes).
- Ignoring substitutes and "do nothing" — often the real competitor.
- Treating a white-space corner as opportunity without demand evidence.
- Stale pricing/feature data — competitors change pages monthly; date every capture.
