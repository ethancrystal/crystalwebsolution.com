# Revision and Self-Editing — where writing actually happens

Depth reference for `[[general-purpose-writer]]`. The draft is raw material; the work is the rewrite. Read this when polishing anything that matters.

## The core truth
First drafts are for discovering what you're saying. Revision is for making it land. Treat the two as different jobs with different mindsets: draft hot and fast for momentum; revise cold and slow with a knife. Never edit while drafting — it strangles the piece before it breathes.

## Passes (do them separately — each pass has one job)
1. **Structure pass.** Is there a single governing idea? Does the order build? Is the real opening buried three sentences down? Does the close resonate or just stop? Cut or move whole paragraphs. Macro before micro — don't polish a sentence you're about to delete.
2. **Clarity pass.** Does each sentence say one thing clearly? Find every place the reader could misread or lose the thread. Replace abstraction with the concrete. Make sure every pronoun has an unambiguous owner.
3. **Compression pass.** Cut 10% minimum. Kill throat-clearing openers, hedges ("somewhat," "rather," "arguably"), redundant pairs ("each and every"), and any summary that restates what the prose showed. If a sentence can lose three words and survive, it must.
4. **Verb-and-diction pass.** Hunt weak verb + adverb pairs and replace with one strong verb. Cut "very/really/quite." Swap fashionable filler (utilize, leverage, delve, robust) for plain words. Make every verb specific.
5. **Rhythm pass.** Read it aloud (or sub-vocalize every word). Mark where you stumble, run out of breath, or drone. Vary sentence length. Break up parallel monotony. Move strong words to the ends of sentences.
6. **Voice-and-tone pass.** Is the register consistent? Does it sound like a person with a point of view, or like fluent nothing? Add temperature where it's gone cold.
7. **Proof pass.** Grammar, spelling, punctuation, names, facts. Last, separately — proofing earlier wastes effort on text you'll cut.

## Read-aloud test
The single most powerful self-editing tool. Your ear catches what your eye forgives: clumsy rhythm, repeated words, breath you can't take, a clause that collapses. If you stumble reading it, the reader will too. Fix until it flows.

## Kill your darlings
The line you're proudest of is often the one serving *you*, not the piece — a clever turn that distracts, a beautiful sentence that doesn't fit. If it doesn't earn its place in *this* piece, cut it (paste it somewhere for later if you must). Vanity is the enemy of the work.

## Line-edit checklist (the micro pass)
- [ ] **Openers:** no "It is important to note," "In today's world," "When it comes to," "There is/are…" starting a sentence that could start with its subject.
- [ ] **Hedges cut:** arguably, somewhat, rather, quite, perhaps-as-filler, "in many ways," "to some extent."
- [ ] **Adverbs:** each one justified or folded into a stronger verb.
- [ ] **Weak verbs:** "is/are/was/were/has/makes/does" doing work a real verb should do.
- [ ] **Passive voice:** only where the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant.
- [ ] **Nominalizations:** "make a decision" → "decide"; "provide an explanation" → "explain."
- [ ] **Filler phrases:** "the fact that," "in order to" → "to," "due to the fact that" → "because."
- [ ] **Repetition:** the same word/root reappearing nearby (unless deliberate); the same sentence-shape three times running.
- [ ] **Clichés and dead metaphors:** refreshed or removed.
- [ ] **Strong words at sentence ends;** weak words buried mid-sentence.
- [ ] **Pronoun clarity:** every "it/this/that/they" points to exactly one thing.
- [ ] **Tense and POV:** consistent unless shifted on purpose.

## Diagnosing "this reads like AI"
When prose feels machine-made, it's almost always some mix of: relentless even-handedness (no view), mechanical parallel structures, hedging, abstraction without concrete grounding, list-itis where prose should flow, tidy tricolons, inflated diction, and a flat uniform sentence rhythm. The fix: **take a position, get specific and sensory, vary the music, and cut the hedges.** Add a real voice. Risk one sentence that a cautious editor might question — that's usually the one that makes it human.

## Knowing when to stop
Revision has diminishing returns and a point past which you sand off the life. Stop when: changes are lateral (swapping equally-good words), the piece does its job for its reader, and it reads cleanly aloud. Done and alive beats perfect and embalmed.
