# Forms and Registers — matching craft to the job

Depth reference for `[[general-purpose-writer]]`. Read when you're working in a specific form and need its laws before reaching for beauty. Craft is universal; its *application* is not. Each form rewards different moves.

## The essay (personal / argumentative)
- **Spine:** one governing idea the whole piece circles and complicates. An essay *thinks*; it doesn't just inform.
- **Open** in the concrete — a scene, an anecdote, a sharp claim — not a definition. **Close** by turning the idea, not summarizing it.
- **Earn authority** through specifics and honest reasoning, not assertion. Acknowledge the strongest counter-case, then answer it — that's strength, not the reflexive both-sides hedge.
- Models: Baldwin (moral heat), Didion (cool precision), Orwell (plain force), Montaigne (drift and return).

## Narrative nonfiction / reportage
- **Scene over summary.** Render moments; let facts arrive inside action. The reader should *watch*, not be briefed.
- **Detail does the arguing.** Choose particulars that carry the theme without your having to state it.
- **Structure is editorial.** What you put first, what you withhold, where you cut — that's where meaning is made.

## Fiction and scene
- **Conflict and desire** drive everything; a character who wants nothing creates no scene.
- **Dialogue** reveals character and advances tension; it is not transcription. Cut the hellos. Use "said" — it's invisible; resist said-bookisms ("he expostulated"). Let subtext carry the weight.
- **Show interiority through behavior and sensation,** not narrated emotion-labels.
- **Scene vs. summary:** dramatize what matters in real time; summarize to bridge. Pacing is the ratio between them.

## Marketing / brand copy
- **One idea, one action.** Know the single thing the reader should do and the single feeling that moves them to it.
- **Benefit over feature,** but grounded — what changes in the reader's life. Concrete and specific outsells superlative and vague every time.
- **Voice is the brand.** Distinct, consistent, human. Cut corporate throat-clearing entirely.
- **Rhythm sells:** short, confident lines; a fragment for punch. But never let cleverness obscure the offer or the next step. Pairs with `[[website-designer]]` (tone) and `[[market-research-expert]]` (audience language).

## Speech / spoken word
- **Write for the ear and the breath.** Short clauses, repetition, parallel structure, a refrain. The listener can't reread — clarity and rhythm are everything.
- **Anaphora and the rule of three** are spoken-word workhorses (used with restraint).
- **Land applause/pause lines** at the ends of sentences and sections. Mark where the speaker breathes.

## Criticism / review
- **A clear judgment, argued from evidence** in the work itself. Quote and point.
- **Describe before you judge;** show you saw the thing clearly, then evaluate.
- **Situate** the work in its tradition or moment without burying the reader in context. Wit is welcome; cruelty for sport is not.

## Long-form post / explainer
- **Earn the scroll.** A strong hook, then a promise of value kept quickly. Front-load the payoff.
- **Structure for skim and depth both:** meaningful subheads, but prose that flows when read straight.
- **One voice, plain and confident.** This is where machine-prose tells (hedging, list-itis, tidy balance) are most fatal — and most common. Beat them.

## Poetry (when asked)
- **Compression and image** above all; the line break is a unit of meaning and timing.
- **Sound** — assonance, consonance, internal rhyme, meter — does heavy lifting. Read aloud relentlessly.
- **Avoid abstraction and the greeting-card register.** Concrete image, fresh turn, earned feeling.

## Register ladder (pick deliberately, shift only for effect)
1. **Intimate** — to one person, unguarded. Contractions, fragments, the second person.
2. **Casual** — conversational, warm, plain.
3. **Neutral/standard** — clear, unmarked; most explanatory writing.
4. **Formal** — measured, fuller syntax, fewer contractions; ceremony and authority.
5. **Elevated/literary** — heightened diction, figurative, rhythmic; reserved for moments that bear the weight.

A sudden drop or jump on this ladder — a plain blunt word in elevated prose, or a single formal note in casual writing — is one of the most reliable tools for emphasis. Use it consciously; an *accidental* shift just reads as a mistake.
