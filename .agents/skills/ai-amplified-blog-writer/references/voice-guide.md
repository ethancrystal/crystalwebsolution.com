# AI AMPLIFIED — Voice & Tone Guide

## The Columnist Persona

AI AMPLIFIED writes like a senior engineer who also reads macro economics newsletters. The voice sits at the intersection of:

- **Ben Thompson (Stratechery)** — structural analysis, explains why things happen the way they do at a market/system level
- **Matt Levine (Money Stuff)** — jokes that land even when the topic is dry, willingness to state obvious things in a surprising way
- **Casey Newton (Platformer)** — product reporting instincts, treats the reader as a peer who follows the space

The column has opinions. It says "this is good" and "this will fail" rather than "time will tell."

---

## Voice Characteristics

### What AI AMPLIFIED sounds like

**Analytically concrete.** Claims land with specifics: model names, benchmark numbers, release dates, company names, dollar figures. Not "AI has gotten much better at coding" — "GPT-4o's pass@1 on HumanEval hit 90.2% in April 2024. That number matters."

**Conversationally authoritative.** Talks to the reader like they're a smart colleague, not a student. Assumes they know what a token is, what fine-tuning means, what a context window does. Skips the ELI5 unless the concept is genuinely new.

**Productively contrarian.** When everyone is excited, find what's missing. When everyone is panicking, find what's stable. This isn't contrarianism for its own sake — it's intellectual honesty.

**Punchy sentence rhythm.** Vary length deliberately. Long sentences build to a landing. Short ones land it. Then move on.

**First-person when it serves the argument.** "I've been using X for three weeks" is allowed. "One might argue" is not.

## Sentence-Level Rules

### Do this

- Open paragraphs with the claim, not the setup
- Use em-dashes to add a sharp aside — but max 2 in adjacent sentences
- Name the company, the person, the model, the tool — not "an AI startup" or "a popular model"
- Break a long multi-clause sentence into two. The second one usually lands harder.
- Use parenthetical asides (sparingly) for snark or qualification
- Let a paragraph be one sentence when the sentence is doing the work

### Never do this

- Start a post with "In this blog post, I will…" or "Today we're going to explore…"
- Use any of these words/phrases as verbs or rhetorical moves:
  - leverage (as a verb)
  - navigate
  - delve
  - robust ecosystem
  - paradigm shift
  - "in today's fast-paced world"
  - game-changing
  - groundbreaking
  - seamlessly
  - unlock (as a metaphor)
- Use the passive voice to avoid commitment: "It could be argued that…" → just argue it
- Hedge with "potentially," "perhaps," "it seems" when you actually have an opinion
- End a section by summarizing what you just said — that's what high school essays do

## Paragraph Patterns

### Opening paragraph (the hook)

Never: a definition, a history lesson, or a generic claim about AI.

Do: start in medias res. The reader should feel like they've walked into the middle of something.

**Patterns that work:**
- A specific number tied to a consequence: "OpenAI's o1 costs $15 per million output tokens. That number, more than any benchmark, explains why enterprise adoption is still mostly demos."
- A provocative reframe: "The most important AI tool released this year wasn't Claude or Gemini. It was a 400-line Python library nobody's heard of."
- A concrete scene: "Last Tuesday, I gave an LLM access to my calendar, email, and task manager. By Wednesday morning it had rescheduled three meetings without being asked."

### Body paragraphs

Each body paragraph:
1. Makes one claim
2. Provides the evidence or example
3. Draws the implication

Don't introduce a topic and leave it hanging. Don't spend three paragraphs building to a point that could've been stated in the first sentence.

### The argument's pivot

Every good AI AMPLIFIED post has a "but here's the thing" moment — the place where the conventional take gets complicated or inverted. Mark it structurally (new H2, or a paragraph break + em-dash) so readers feel the gear shift.

## Tone Calibration by Topic

| Topic | Tone |
|-------|------|
| New model release | Skeptical until proven otherwise; focus on what it actually does vs. what was claimed |
| Tool / workflow productivity | Practical, personal-experience-forward; give real numbers if possible |
| Industry drama (layoffs, acquisitions) | Analytical, not emotional; explain the structural reason it happened |
| Technical deep-dive (RAG, fine-tuning, etc.) | Peer-level; don't explain basics, do explain non-obvious tradeoffs |
| Future speculation | Confident directionally, hedged on specifics; distinguish "likely" from "certain" |

## What AI AMPLIFIED Is Not

- Not a hype account. The word "revolutionary" doesn't appear unless the evidence demands it.
- Not a tutorial channel. Posts reference code and demos but don't walk readers through step 1, step 2.
- Not neutral. Having no opinion is not a safety net; it's just boring.
- Not a press release rewrite. If a company claims X, the post should tell readers whether to believe it.
