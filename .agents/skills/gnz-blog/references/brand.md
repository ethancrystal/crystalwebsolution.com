## 1. The company in one paragraph

GNZ Enterprises is a digital marketing solutions agency that helps businesses grow through measurable, integrated digital programs — SEO, paid media, social, web design, email, content, analytics, and AI-driven marketing. The agency works across industries with small-to-mid-sized businesses, e-commerce brands, B2B service companies, and growth-stage startups. The positioning is **execution over theory** — GNZ ships work that produces measurable outcomes, and the blog is the proof-of-craft surface.

## 2. Positioning pillars

The three positions every GNZ blog post should reinforce, implicitly or explicitly:

**1. Specificity over hand-waving.** Most agency blogs talk about "growth," "engagement," and "transformation" without naming the tactic or the number. GNZ posts name the tactic and the number. *"We changed the meta description to lead with the user benefit instead of the keyword and CTR went from 1.8% to 3.2% over six weeks."*

**2. Integrated, not siloed.** Most agencies sell channels in isolation — "we do SEO," "we do paid." GNZ's edge is that the channels work together. Posts should reference how SEO informs paid (which keywords convert), how paid informs SEO (search query data is content gold), how email informs both (segmentation telegraphs which audiences are most valuable).

**3. AI-fluent, not AI-credulous.** GNZ uses modern AI tools where they actually help — content production at scale, ad creative variations, personalization, programmatic media buying, analytics. But the agency does *not* sell "AI marketing" as a product, because most "AI marketing" pitches are vapor. Posts should treat AI as a capability used selectively, not as a magic answer.

## 3. Voice — what GNZ sounds like

**Confident but not arrogant.** The reader is a smart person who has been burned by agencies before. The voice has to earn trust rather than declare it. No "we're the best." Yes "here's how this works, here's what we did, here's what happened."

**Plain English first.** Define jargon the first time it's used. *Customer acquisition cost (CAC) — what you spend to get one new customer.* The reader who already knows the term reads past it; the reader who doesn't is included rather than excluded.

**Direct, with one beat of warmth.** GNZ posts are not corporate-formal and not over-casual. The voice is what a competent senior marketer sounds like talking to another business owner over coffee — direct, specific, occasionally dry, never preachy.

**Numbers are the rhetorical engine.** Specific percentages, specific dollar amounts, specific timeframes. "Cut CAC by 31% in 60 days" is a sentence the reader believes. "Significantly improved efficiency" is filler.

**Position-taking when warranted.** GNZ posts say what they think. *"Most CRO 'best practices' are derived from a handful of SaaS A/B tests and don't apply to e-commerce. Here's what actually works."* The blog is a place to disagree with the industry, not parrot it.

**No bullshit detector clauses.** No "of course, every business is different" hedges that drain conviction. If GNZ thinks the answer is *yes, do X*, the post says *yes, do X* — and names the conditions where the rule breaks at the end.

### Voice fingerprint phrases

These are the recurring moves a reader should start to associate with GNZ blog content:

- *"Here's what actually works:"* — used to pivot from problem to specific solution
- *"The honest answer is:"* — used when most agencies would hedge
- *"In our experience —"* — anchors a claim to first-hand knowledge
- *"Two things matter:"* — sets up a tight, dual-emphasis frame (and avoids the AI triple-parallel reflex)
- *"This is where most teams get it wrong:"* — sets up a contrarian or corrective point
- *"What this looks like in practice:"* — pivots from concept to example

Use these deliberately, not as ticks. Two or three per post is voice; ten is a parody.

## 5. Audience personas

GNZ writes for four primary reader personas. The skill should identify which persona the current post is aimed at and tune the voice/depth accordingly.

### Persona A — The small business owner (45% of audience)
- 35–60 years old, founder or owner of a 5–50 person business
- Service-based or product-based — landscaping, dental, accounting, e-commerce, restaurants, professional services
- Marketing knowledge: low to moderate. Knows what an ad is; does not know what a UTM parameter is
- Pain points: "I'm spending money on marketing but I don't know what's working." "My website doesn't bring in leads." "I tried a freelancer and got burned."
- What they want from a blog post: explanation in plain English, real examples, a sense of "here's what I should do Monday morning"
- CTA that converts: free strategy call, free audit, downloadable checklist

### Persona B — The in-house marketing manager (25%)
- 25–40 years old, marketing manager or director at a 10–500 person company
- Knows the landscape; needs to make case to executives, manage execution, hit numbers
- Pain points: stretched thin across channels, needs a partner to scale specific functions, executive pressure to prove ROI
- What they want: tactical specifics, frameworks they can adapt, comparison content ("agency vs. in-house," "Tool A vs. Tool B")
- CTA that converts: capability deck, case studies in their industry, "how we work" page

### Persona C — The e-commerce founder (15%)
- 28–45 years old, founder of a DTC brand doing $500K–$10M/year
- Marketing knowledge: high but uneven (deep in paid, light in SEO; or vice versa)
- Pain points: paid CAC is up; iOS 14.5 broke attribution; need omnichannel growth
- What they want: e-commerce-specific tactics, Klaviyo/Shopify-specific guidance, real DTC case studies
- CTA that converts: DTC-specific case study, free Shopify audit, paid + email integrated strategy call

### Persona D — The B2B SaaS or service founder (15%)
- 30–45 years old, founder/CEO of a B2B SaaS or professional services firm
- Marketing knowledge: moderate; growing past the founder-led growth phase
- Pain points: SEO is slow but compounding; paid is expensive and short-lived; need a content + SEO + LinkedIn + email machine that builds the brand
- What they want: long-term content strategy, B2B-specific paid tactics, LinkedIn organic + ABM frameworks
- CTA that converts: B2B-specific strategy call, content strategy audit, ABM playbook download

## 7. Style and formatting standards

- **Headlines:** Title Case for blog headlines. Sentence case for H2 subheaders
- **Numbers:** Spell out one through nine; numerals for 10+. Always numerals for percentages, dollar amounts, and measurements
- **Percentages:** *47%* not *47 percent*. *Increased CTR by 47%* not *Increased CTR by forty-seven percent*
- **Acronyms:** Spell out on first use with the acronym in parentheses. *Customer acquisition cost (CAC).* After that, the acronym only
- **Brand & product names:** Always capitalized correctly — *Google Ads*, *Meta*, *Shopify*, *Klaviyo*, *HubSpot*, *Webflow*. Not *google ads* or *Hubspot*
- **Dates:** *January 15, 2026* in body; *01/15/2026* in tables. Use the year when the post is dated content (yearly guides, trend pieces)
- **Currency:** *$5,000* not *$5000* or *5,000 dollars*
- **Em dashes:** With no spaces around them — like this. Used for interruption, not as default pauses
- **Oxford comma:** Yes
- **Voice:** Active. *We launched the campaign.* Not *the campaign was launched by us*

## 8. CTA library

Standard CTAs and where they go. Customize the link destinations to match GNZ's actual landing pages.

**Top of post — never use.** CTAs at the top before value is delivered are the #1 reader-trust killer.

**After a value-delivery section** (typically after explaining a framework, sharing a case-study outcome, or completing a how-to step):

> Want help applying this to your business? [**Book a free strategy call →**](https://gnzenterprises.vercel.app/contact)

**After the cost / comparison section:**

> Curious what an integrated digital marketing program would look like for your business? [**Get a custom proposal →**](https://gnzenterprises.vercel.app/contact)

**End of post (conclusion section):**

> If you're ready to move from guessing to a measurable digital growth plan — [**talk to our team →**](https://gnzenterprises.vercel.app/contact)

**Service-specific** (when the post is about a single service):

> See how GNZ's [SEO / paid media / web design / etc.] services work → [**Explore our [service]**](https://gnzenterprises.vercel.app/services)

## 9. Banned topics and angles

- "Why every business needs marketing" — too broad, no SEO value, no conversion value
- "The future of marketing" pieces without a specific prediction
- "X tips for Y" listicles where the tips are unranked and obvious
- Anything that requires us to claim expertise we don't have (e.g., a piece about pharma compliance if we don't serve pharma)
- Anything that disparages a specific competitor by name (industry-wide critique is fine; named takedowns are not)
- Hot takes on contested political topics not relevant to digital marketing

## 10. Required disclaimers

For pieces touching on results, ROI, or financial claims, include this disclaimer block at the end:

> *Results vary based on industry, market conditions, audience, budget, and execution. Case studies and examples referenced are illustrative of approach, not a guarantee of outcomes. Past performance does not predict future results.*

For pieces touching on legal, tax, or compliance topics, include:

> *This article is for educational purposes only and does not constitute legal, financial, or tax advice. For advice specific to your situation, consult a licensed professional.*
