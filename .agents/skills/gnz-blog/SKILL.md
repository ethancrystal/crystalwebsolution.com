# GNZ Blog

A protocol for writing blog content for GNZ Enterprises — a digital marketing solutions agency — at a quality that actually ranks, actually converts, and actually sounds like a real expert rather than a generic content mill.

## Why this exists

Most digital marketing agency blogs read like they were written by a junior intern who has read three Neil Patel posts and confused fluency with expertise. The same five tips. The same screenshot of a Google Analytics dashboard. The same "In today's competitive digital landscape…" opening that signals to the reader: *you are about to read filler*.

GNZ's blog has to do the opposite. Three jobs at once:

1. **Rank in Google** for high-intent keywords that bring in qualified leads
2. **Build expert authority** so readers trust GNZ enough to inquire
3. **Convert readers into clients** via embedded CTAs that feel earned, not begged

A blog post that does all three is rare. A blog post that does *none* is the default. This skill exists to ship the first kind reliably.

## The protocol

Execute in order. The user sees a brief trace, then the post.

### Phase 1 — Brief inventory

Before drafting, name:

- **Topic** — the actual subject, in one sentence
- **Format** — SEO pillar, how-to guide, case study, thought-leadership op-ed, listicle, news commentary, founder essay (formats in `references/blog-templates.md`)
- **Target keyword** — what should this rank for? If the user didn't specify, propose one and confirm
- **Primary audience** — small-business owner, in-house marketer, agency-curious enterprise, e-commerce founder, B2B SaaS founder (full personas in `references/brand.md`)
- **Funnel stage** — top-of-funnel (educational, broad), middle (comparison, "how to choose"), bottom (services-led, conversion-focused)
- **Length target** — pillar 2,500–4,000, how-to 1,200–2,000, case study 800–1,500, op-ed 700–1,000, listicle 1,200–2,500
- **CTA destination** — homepage, services page, contact form, free audit, specific landing page. Default to "Book a free strategy call" linking to the contact form when unspecified
- **Existing brand voice cues** — if the user has published before, check tone. If not, default to GNZ voice as defined in `references/brand.md`

If anything material is unclear, ask. Otherwise infer and proceed.

### Phase 2 — Angle and thesis

A blog post that is just a "topic" is filler. A blog post with an angle is content.

- For **how-tos**, the angle is the *specific approach* — "the 90-day SEO sprint method," not "how to do SEO"
- For **pillars**, the angle is the *contrarian or comprehensive framing* — "everything you need to know about [X] *in 2026*, including what's changed since AI Overviews"
- For **case studies**, the angle is the *non-obvious lesson* — what worked that wasn't supposed to, or what didn't work the way the playbook said it would
- For **thought-leadership**, the angle is the *position other agencies aren't taking* — agency blogs that sound like every other agency blog don't build authority

In the trace, state the angle in one sentence. Test: does it sharpen the topic into something a reader would *prefer* to alternatives, or is it just the topic restated?

### Phase 3 — Format and template

Pick the format. Each has a worked template in `references/blog-templates.md`:

- **SEO pillar** (2,500–4,000 words) — for category-level keywords ("complete guide to local SEO," "what is conversion rate optimization")
- **How-to guide** (1,200–2,000 words) — for tactical, action-oriented keywords ("how to set up Google Ads conversion tracking")
- **Case study** (800–1,500 words) — for proof, social validation, and bottom-funnel content
- **Industry op-ed** (700–1,000 words) — for thought leadership on news, trends, controversies
- **Listicle / roundup** (1,200–2,500 words) — for comparison and "best of" queries
- **Founder / agency essay** (1,200–3,500 words) — for brand-building, on the GNZ leadership voice

Read the relevant template before drafting.

### Phase 4 — Architect

Sketch in the trace:

- **Headline** — the working headline. Should contain the target keyword and a value-promise modifier (year, number, "complete," "for beginners," "in 2026"). Test against a SERP for the target keyword
- **Meta title** — 55–60 characters, primary keyword first, brand last
- **Meta description** — 150–160 characters, summary plus a soft CTA
- **URL slug** — short, hyphenated, primary keyword included, year only if dated content
- **Opening hook** — specific scenarios (3 in short sentences) or one named situation. Never "In today's fast-paced world."
- **Section H2s** — 5 to 10 of them. Each H2 is a beat in the argument, not a "point to cover"
- **CTAs** — where they go (always inline, usually 2–3 per post: one mid-article after value is delivered, one in the conclusion, optionally a third after a price/comparison section)
- **Internal links** — what other GNZ posts/pages this links to (drives crawl equity)
- **Outbound authority links** — 2–4 to recognizable sources (Google, HubSpot research, government data, industry studies)

### Phase 5 — Stress-test

Before drafting, attack the plan:

- **SERP check.** What's currently ranking for this keyword? If the top 3 results are listicles, a listicle is likely the right format. If they're pillars, write a sharper pillar. Don't write the format that lost
- **Skim test.** A reader who only reads headlines + H2s + bold lead-ins — do they get the value? If no, the architecture is hiding the goods
- **"Why GNZ" test.** Could this post have been written by any agency? If yes, find the angle that makes it GNZ's (a specific framework, a client example, a contrarian take)
- **Conversion test.** Does the post earn the CTA, or does the CTA appear out of nowhere? CTAs should follow value delivery — never replace it

If the plan fails, revise (announce "Revision:" in trace) before drafting.

### Phase 6 — Draft

Voice and craft principles (see `references/brand.md` for full GNZ voice; the craft principles below are inherited from the `award-columnist` skill where deeper detail lives):

- **Verbs do the work.** *We launched* beats *we conducted the launch process*. *It converted* beats *it drove conversion*
- **Specifics over generics.** "Increased revenue by 47% in 90 days" beats "drove significant revenue growth." Real client outcomes (anonymized if needed) beat hypothetical examples
- **Concrete examples over abstract principles.** Show the Google Ads dashboard scenario, the actual email subject line that worked, the specific landing-page change. The post earns trust by demonstrating expertise, not asserting it
- **Bold lead-ins for sub-points.** Each scannable point starts with a 2-5 word bold phrase, then prose. This is the format that reads cleanly without bullets
- **One specific industry example per section.** Don't just say "for e-commerce brands" — say "an e-commerce brand selling premium skincare in the $50–$150 range." The detail earns the credibility
- **Plain English for technical terms.** Define jargon the first time. *Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO) — making more visitors take action without spending more on traffic*
- **No throat-clearing.** Delete "In this post, we'll discuss…" Start with the hook

### Phase 7 — Cut

The first draft is always 15–25% too long.

- Any sentence that doesn't add information, voice, or pacing — gone
- Hedges ("perhaps," "arguably," "may possibly") — gone unless doing real epistemic work
- Adjectives that aren't pulling weight ("amazing," "incredible," "powerful") — gone
- Three-item lists when two would do — gone
- The first paragraph, often. The real opening is usually one paragraph in
- Stock phrases ("at the end of the day," "needless to say," "it goes without saying") — gone

### Phase 8 — Tighten

Composition pass. For each sentence:

- Verb early enough that the reader doesn't lose the spine
- Nominalizations reversed (*make a decision* → *decide*)
- No three sentences in a row of the same length or same opening word
- Em-dashes for interruption only, not as default pauses
- Read the CTA aloud — does it sound like something you'd say, or like marketing-speak?
- Check the headline against the SERP one more time

### Phase 9 — SEO and CTA hardening

Before delivering:

- **Target keyword density.** Primary keyword in: title (early), H1, first paragraph, one or two H2s, meta description, slug, alt text of images. Don't stuff — natural placement
- **Related keywords.** Include 3–5 LSI keywords naturally (synonyms, related terms; e.g., for "SEO" — search engine optimization, organic traffic, Google ranking, search visibility)
- **Internal links.** Minimum 2 to other GNZ content (other blogs, services pages, contact)
- **External authority links.** 2–4 to recognized sources
- **Image suggestions block.** Hero + 1 supporting infographic concept + 1 process diagram + alt text for each
- **Schema notes.** Article schema mandatory; FAQPage schema if there's an FAQ; HowTo schema if it's a how-to format
- **CTA discipline.** 2–3 inline CTAs at value-delivery moments. Never at the top. Never one-after-another
- **Disclaimer block.** For pieces that touch on results or financial claims, add "Results vary based on industry, market conditions, and execution. The case studies referenced are illustrative."

## Anti-patterns — kill on sight

Phrases and habits that mark a post as generic agency filler. Avoid with hostility.

**Banned phrases:** "in today's fast-paced world," "in today's digital age," "ever-evolving landscape," "navigate the complexities," "delve into," "unlock the power," "harness the potential," "synergy," "leverage" (as verb), "robust ecosystem," "paradigm shift," "best-in-class," "world-class solutions," "industry-leading," "cutting-edge," "transformative," "game-changing," "revolutionary," "next-generation," "seamless," "holistic approach," "thought leader," "moving the needle," "low-hanging fruit," "circle back," "deep dive," "going forward," "at the end of the day," "needless to say."

**Banned openings:**
- "In today's [adjective] world…"
- "Did you know that [statistic with no source]…"
- "[Topic] is more important than ever"
- "Have you ever wondered…"
- A dictionary definition of the topic ("SEO, short for Search Engine Optimization, is…")

**Banned CTA patterns:**
- "Contact us today!" with no context
- A CTA in the first 200 words
- A CTA that promises something the article didn't demonstrate
- More than 3 CTAs in a post under 2,500 words
- Stacked CTAs (two CTAs in a row, separated only by a line)

**Structural anti-patterns:**
- The "5 Ways to…" listicle where the 5 ways are obvious and unranked
- The case study with no specific numbers
- The how-to with no screenshots, examples, or specific tools named
- The pillar that doesn't define key terms early
- The thought-leadership piece that takes no position

## The principle

The GNZ blog has to earn the reader's next click — to another blog post, to a services page, or to the contact form. Every choice should be made with that in mind: the headline that makes them open the post, the opening that makes them keep reading, the specific example that makes them trust the expertise, the inline CTA that arrives at the moment of maximum interest, the kicker that resolves the post and points to what's next.

Generic agency content fails because it tries to look authoritative without demonstrating expertise. GNZ content succeeds by *showing the work* — specific frameworks, specific client outcomes (anonymized if needed), specific tools, specific tactical sequences. Trust is built through specificity, not adjectives.

If the trace shows a "topic" rather than an "angle," or the headline is a generic listicle, or the post could have been written by any agency — go back and sharpen. The skill failed if the draft is interchangeable with a Neil Patel post from 2019.

Bland is the enemy. Generic agency voice is the enemy. The job is to write the post the prospective client reads, finishes, and inquires from.
